Field notes

Lessons from the right seat

Truck GPS is not car GPS

A 13 ft 6 in trailer needs routing that knows about bridge clearances, weight limits and truck-restricted roads. Ordinary car navigation doesn’t — and the low bridge doesn’t care what your phone said. As useful as any GPS is, it’s a tool, not the decision-maker — the driver still has to look, think and choose.

Hours of service in one breath

Inside a 14-hour on-duty window you can drive up to 11 hours total — but you have to take a 30-minute break before you complete 8 hours of driving, and that break is what unlocks the remaining 3 of the 11. On top of the daily limits there’s the 70-hour clock: 60 hours on duty in 7 days, or 70 in 8, depending on the carrier’s schedule. Sleeper-berth splits add flexibility. The rules get revised, so check current FMCSA guidance.

Brake before the curve, not in it

The slowest part of any curve should be the beginning. Get your speed down before you turn in — you can always speed up through a curve, but scrubbing off speed once you’re already leaned into it is how trailers roll.

Parking is part of the plan

A 53-foot trailer limits where you can stop. Plan your parking and your hours together, because the legal place to run out of hours is rarely where you want to be.

General information from field experience — not legal advice. Always verify current federal and state rules.

Prime Inc. is a training company. Bring an existing CDL, or come in knowing nothing about trucking and be taught how to pass the test and run your own truck from scratch — either way, give the recruiter my driver code so you get connected to me.

I’m an independent contractor driving a leased truck with Prime, and I’m a certified trainer for drivers coming into the company. Trainees ride with me anywhere from 10,000 to 30,000 miles, depending on the training team’s rules, and in that time I teach them whatever they need to successfully run their own truck. Every trainee is different: some are kids who’ve never driven anything bigger than a car, just out of their parents’ house for the first time; others have 30+ years behind the wheel and are coming back to Prime after letting their CDL lapse.
