Backing, simplified

Three patterns to master

Almost every dock, lot and truck stop comes down to one of these three.

Top views with the dock at the top. Solid rig = where you start. Gold arrow = the path the rear of the trailer takes while backing. Dashed outline = where the trailer ends up. Faint outlines = positions along the way. The paths come from a simple vehicle model of a 53-ft trailer behind a tractor sized like the rig on the Trucking page; the parked trailers sit on 12-ft door spacing. Teaching sketches — not a substitute for in-person coaching.

How a session runs

Show it. Say it. Do it. Review it.

  1. Get out and look

    Walk the space. Know what’s behind, beside and above before moving an inch.

  2. Say the plan

    Name the pivot point and the path out loud. A plan you can say is a plan you can fix.

  3. Do the reps

    Repeat until the set-up is automatic. Pull up whenever it goes sideways — that’s a skill, not a failure.

  4. Debrief

    One honest takeaway per attempt: what worked, what changes next time.
